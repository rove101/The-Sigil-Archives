# Mutation Checks

When a Mutation check is required, the character must make a Constitution saving throw, with a [DC](https://5e.tools/variantrules.html#difficulty%20class_xphb) equal to 8 plus the character's level. The more power flowing through a creature, the more likely they are to succumb to mutation.

On a failed save, the character gains 1 [Corruption](https://5e.tools/conditionsdiseases.html#corruption_grimhollowcg24) level and rolls 1d20 on the [Great Beast Mutations](https://5e.tools/tables.html#great%20beast%20mutations_grimhollowcg24) table. If the result is a mutation that character already has, ignore the result and roll again.

A Mutation lasts until the character has no [Corruption](https://5e.tools/conditionsdiseases.html#corruption_grimhollowcg24) levels, or until they finish a [[Resting#Long Rest|Long Rest]].

# Curses' Effect on Bodily Autonomy

Some rules and effects specifically call for a Mutation check. In addition, the GM can require a Mutation check whenever a character is exposed to powerful magic, curses, creatures, or other effects that put them in contact with eldritch horrors or unknowable forces.

Here are some examples of when a Mutation check could be relevant and evocative:

- When a character fails a saving throw to avoid an effect created by a powerful creature who uses or was created by transformative magic.
- When a caster loses Concentration on a spell.
- When a creature would receive a condition it already has.
- When a deal with a powerful immortal being is broken.
- When a creature from the Material Plane travels travels to another plane of existence.
- When a shocking or devastating event occurs outside of combat.
- When a character with two failed Death Saving Throws succeeds on a Death Saving Throw.
- When a creature is targeted by a harmful spell such as Curse.
